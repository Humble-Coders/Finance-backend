# [M4] Stop counting money moved between your own accounts as income or spending

## 📖 Story / Why

Home's income, spending and net, the budget, and the Money Health Score all start from one
question: what came in and what went out. Today the answer counts money that only moved
between the household's own accounts, as if it had arrived or been spent.

Two cases do the most harm:

- **A credit-card bill.** Spend $1,000 on the card, pay it from chequing, import both statements:
  - spending shows **$2,000**: the purchases plus the payment;
  - income shows **$1,000 too much**, because the card's "PAYMENT – THANK YOU" line is money
    arriving;
  - "Where your money went" shows the same $1,000 twice.
- **Saving.** Moving $500 into savings is filed as `savings` and counted as **spending**. The net
  figure and the score's savings-rate part *drop* when someone saves. That is exactly the
  behaviour the app should reward.

The household sees figures it knows are wrong, and the score punishes good habits. This ticket
makes money moving between the household's own accounts count as neither income nor spending.

## 🧭 Context

- Repo: `Finance-backend`. **No brief** — manager decision, 2026-10-09. PRD **F3** (dashboard),
  **F4** (budget), **F5** (health score). The app side is **4.7** (`FinAI-Mobile-2026`).
- **How it works today:**
  - `countable()` in `app/services/dashboard.py:258` decides which rows a total may include.
    `_sums_by_direction` and `figures_by_month` then add up **every** credit as income and
    **every** debit as expenses, whatever the category.
  - `MonthFigures.net` is income − expenses. The score's `savings_consistency` reads
    `net / income` (`app/services/health_score.py:184`).
  - The budget already leaves `income` and `transfers` out of its lines (`NOT_BUDGETABLE`,
    `app/services/budget.py:73`). The dashboard does not.
  - `Account.kind` has `credit_card` (`app/models/enums.py:47`), but no calculation reads it.
  - Duplicate detection (`app/services/ledger.py`) only compares rows **within one account**.
    The bank's payment and the card's payment received are never linked.
  - The categorisation prompt (`app/services/categorization.py:68`) has only one hint: incoming
    amounts are usually `income` or `transfers`. The card's payment credit often comes out as
    `income`.
- **Decisions (manager, 2026-10-09):**
  1. **Leave `transfers` and `savings` out of income and expenses.** This applies everywhere a
     flow is summed:
     - income, expenses and net;
     - the trend and the daily running balance;
     - "Where your money went";
     - `previous_*`.

     Savings deposits and withdrawals still show in the **investments** card (`invested` /
     `withdrawn`), where they belong. `debt_payment` (a real loan or a carried balance) stays
     spending. Unfiled rows (no category) still count, as today.
  2. **Link own-account pairs and file both rows as `transfers`, and also send both to Review**
     so the person confirms. A pair is:
     - a debit on one of the household's accounts and a credit on **another** of its accounts;
     - the same amount and currency;
     - dates within **5 days** of each other (card payments post a few business days apart).

     The new review reason is `own_transfer`. Pairing runs on every import, against rows
     already saved, so it works **whichever statement is imported first**.
  3. **A credit on a `credit_card` account that is the card being paid** (no purchase refund):
     - file it as `transfers` with reason `own_transfer`, even with no partner;
     - its purchases are on the same statement, so counting it as income is always wrong.
  4. **A bank-side card payment with no partner stays counted.** If the card statement was never
     imported, that payment is the only record of the spending, and hiding it would understate
     the month. It is paired when the card statement arrives.
  5. **Health score formula → `v3`.** The calculation is unchanged, but its inputs change, so
     scores before and after are not comparable. `FORMULA_VERSION = "v3"` gives "vs last month"
     the same treatment 5.3 gave v2: hidden across versions.
  6. **A re-parse of an unsaved import is free** (for 4.7). 4.7 adds a step where the person
     checks the rows *before* saving.

     **The problem:** a parsed import already counts against the monthly limit
     (`_imports_this_month`, `app/api/statements.py:101`, which counts every non-failed import).
     If the phone kills the app mid-check, the app reads the file again and the second parse is
     refused with `429 import_quota_exceeded`.

     **The new rule:** when a parse arrives and the household has an import that was parsed but
     never saved (`awaiting_review`, `confirmed_at` null), created in the last **24 hours**:
     - that import is **replaced**: mark it `failed` with `failure_reason = "superseded"`, so it
       stops counting, and the new parse is checked against the limit as if it never existed;
     - **don't delete it**: its diagnostic text, if the person agreed to keep it, has its own
       30-day life;
     - saving a replaced import afterwards is `409 import_superseded` through `log_conflict`, so
       a stale client can never save two statements on one allowance;
     - **at most 3 replacements per household per 24 hours** (configurable), after which the
       normal limit applies. That bounds what the model calls can cost.

     The rule is per household because `statement_import` has no account column. The test
     household exemption is unchanged.
- **Pairing rules worth getting right:**
  - **One partner each.** If a row has several candidates, pair the nearest date. If two are
    equally near, pair **none**: a wrong pair hides real money.
  - **Rows from the same import never pair** (both are on one account).
  - **Never pair a suspected duplicate**, or a row the person has already categorised
    themselves. A person's choice outranks a guess (`CLAUDE.md` → categorisation).
  - Pairing does not merge, delete or change amounts. It only sets the category and the review
    reason. If one side is recategorised later, the other side is left alone.
- **Enum change:** `review_reason` is a native Postgres enum (`SAEnum(..., name="review_reason")`).
  - Adding `own_transfer` needs `ALTER TYPE review_reason ADD VALUE`. That cannot be used in the
    transaction that adds it, and Postgres cannot drop an enum value.
  - The downgrade has to recreate the type: rename it, create the old type, cast the column,
    drop the renamed type. Rows with the new value must be mapped first (to `NULL`, with
    `needs_review` left true).
  - **Test the downgrade in CI.**
- **The app copes already:** `ReviewReason.fromWire` maps an unknown reason to `UNKNOWN` and still
  shows the row for review. 4.7 adds the proper wording.

- **Corrections agreed at `/start-ticket` (2026-10-09)**, after reading the code more closely:
  1. **"Never saved" means no transactions point at the import.** An import stays
     `awaiting_review` after its rows are saved, and `confirmed_at` is only set once the review
     queue is finished (`app/services/review.py:74`). The `confirmed_at` test in decision 6
     would have replaced saved imports that still had rows in review.
  2. **"A row the person categorised themselves"** means one of:
     - the household has a correction rule for the merchant (`learn` records one on every
       recategorise);
     - a hand-typed row whose category the person picked (`chosen`);
     - an earlier hand-typed row that has a category, since nothing records who picked it.
  3. **A card payment is told apart from a refund by the categoriser's answer** (income,
     transfers or other), not by keywords. Keyword lists would be per-language code, and the
     categoriser only ever sees merchant and amount.
  4. **Pairing runs after `file_rows`**, so the categoriser can't overwrite it.
  5. **A pair link column, `transfer_pair_id`** (set on both rows), records "one partner each".
     Without it, a bank payment already paired could be claimed again by another card's payment
     of the same amount.
  6. **Only re-filed rows go to Review.** A side that already fits (Savings stays Savings for the
     investments card; a loan payment stays Debt payment) is linked but not flagged.
  7. **A pair needs evidence**, not just matching amounts: money arriving at a card, loan,
     savings or investment account; money leaving savings or investment; or either side
     already filed as Transfers or Savings. Spending (Groceries, etc.) is never re-filed.

## 🎯 Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **Card bill, both statements, either import order:** $1,000 of purchases on the card, paid
      from chequing. Expenses = $1,000 (not $2,000), income is unchanged by the payment, and
      "Where your money went" shows the $1,000 once. Both payment rows are `transfers` with
      `review_reason = own_transfer`.
- [ ] **Card statement only:** the payment credit is `transfers` + `own_transfer`, and income
      excludes it.
- [ ] **Bank statement only:** the card payment still counts as spending. Importing the card
      statement later pairs it, and the month's figures update.
- [ ] **Savings:** a $500 move to savings does not change expenses or lower net. The savings-rate
      component rises accordingly. The investments card still shows $500 set aside.
- [ ] **No false pairs:**
  - two equally near candidates pair none;
  - rows from one import never pair;
  - a row the person categorised themselves is never re-filed;
  - suspected duplicates are not paired.
- [ ] **Every total uses the same rule:** income, expenses, net, trend, daily balance, previous
      month and the breakdown all agree. A test computes them two ways and compares.
- [ ] **Score:** new snapshots are `v3`; "vs last month" is null across v2 → v3; v1 and v2
      snapshots still load.
- [ ] **Re-parse:**
  - a household with one free import parses, doesn't save, and parses again within 24 hours:
    the second parse succeeds and the first is `failed` / `superseded`;
  - saving the first now returns `409 import_superseded`;
  - a fourth replacement in 24 hours, or a re-parse after a **saved** import, gets the normal
    `429`.
- [ ] Pairing adds **one** query per import, whatever the row count (a test counts statements).
- [ ] The migration applies, reverses and re-applies in CI.
- [ ] `ruff check`, `ruff format --check` and `pytest` pass, and the `database` count grew.

  import lands inside the 5-day window. A one-off backfill is a follow-up, if wanted.
- Any change to the budget generator's maths, beyond reading the corrected spending.
- Investments and debts in the score (formula v3 is only the input change; a debts/buffer
  formula would be a separate ticket).
