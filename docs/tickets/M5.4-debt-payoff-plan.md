# [M5] Your plan: where the money left over goes, debt payoff first

## 📖 Story / Why

People don't only want to know what they spent. They want to know **what to do with what's
left**. Someone with $1,200 left each month, a Visa at 20.99%, a line of credit at 9.5% and a
car loan at 6.9%, who also wants a house deposit and a trip, has the most expensive question in
personal finance in front of them, and nothing in the app answers it.

This ticket builds the answer as plain, testable arithmetic. It is PRD **F11 (Debt Payoff
Optimizer)** plus the "proposes allocations toward savings/debt/goals" half of **F4**. Each
month:
1. **What's left over.** Income minus spending, counted the way Home counts it (#73).
2. **Every debt's minimum** is paid first. It's already inside the budget's Debt line.
3. **A safety buffer** the person chooses.
4. **Extra money goes to the highest-interest debt** (avalanche), or the smallest balance first
   (snowball) if the person prefers. A paid-off debt's payment rolls on to the next one.
5. **Goals** (house, car, travel, electronics…) get the share the person chooses. Below a rate
   line they choose, a debt gets only its minimum, and the money goes to goals and savings
   instead.

The person sees the payoff order, each debt's payoff month, the interest saved compared with
paying only minimums, the date they'll be debt-free, and how the money left over splits.
Balances stay current: typed in, updated by hand, or **read from the card's own statement**.

This is guidance, not advice (PRD §1). The math lives on the server (`CLAUDE.md` → Financial
math); the AI coach (M6) will later explain it, never compute it.

- **The plan's arithmetic** (in a new `app/services/plan.py`; pure functions, `Decimal`, integer
  minor units, as `budget.score()` and `goals.project()` are):
  - **Money left over** = this month's budget **Savings** remainder. It's the same figure the
    Budget tab shows, so the two never disagree. With a shortfall there's nothing to split, and
    the plan says so.
  - **The split, in order:**
    1. The buffer, until its goal reaches the target.
    2. The **goal share** of what remains, to goals: by priority, each up to its
       `required_monthly`, and goals without a date after those.
    3. The rest as **extra debt payment**: debts **above the rate line**, in avalanche order
       (highest rate first) or snowball order (smallest balance first).
    4. Once no debt is above the line, whatever is left is **"Save or invest"**. This is one
       generic bucket. Never name a product or security (PRD §1).
  - **Payoff schedule:** month by month, up to **600 months**.
    - Each month's interest is the balance × annual rate ÷ 12, using the promotional rate until
      it ends.
    - The payment is the minimum, plus the extra for the debt at the front of the order.
    - When a debt is paid off, its whole payment rolls to the next one.
    - Interest is rounded half up to the minor unit each month. State this in the docstring and
      test it.
    - A debt whose payment never covers its interest is reported as **"never paid off at this
      payment"**, not looped forever.
    - A debt with **no rate** goes after the others with "Add the interest rate", never with a
      guessed one.
  - **Outputs:**
    - each debt's order, payoff month and total interest;
    - total interest, and the **interest saved** compared with minimums only;
    - the **debt-free month**;
    - the **snowball comparison** (the extra interest it costs).
- **Linked debts also fix two counting gaps** found in #74's review:
  - **Score:** debt paid counts payments **to the debts the person listed**. A card paid in full
    can't hide a missed loan payment.
  - **Budget:** the Debt line leaves out the minimum of a **card** whose statement is imported,
    because that card's purchases are already budgeted in categories.

  Unlinked debts keep today's behaviour.
- **Statement-reader change:** the prompt and the response shape gain an optional summary,
  `{"closing_balance": "...", "closing_date": "YYYY-MM-DD"}`, read from the STATEMENT HEADER.
  - Never a transaction.
  - Bump `_PROMPT_VERSION`.
  - Keep `LLM_THINKING_BUDGET=0`; it once broke parsing (team memory).
  - The balance is a decimal string, copied exactly like amounts.
  - If it's absent or unreadable it's simply missing, and the import never fails over it.
- **Endpoints** (all household-scoped; another household's id is 404):
  - `GET/POST/PATCH/DELETE /debts`;
  - `GET/PUT /plan/preferences`;
  - `GET /plan?month=YYYY-MM`: money left over, the split, the payoff schedule, totals and the

## 🎯 Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **Avalanche:**
  - with a Visa at 20.99%, a line of credit at 9.5% and a car loan at 6.9%, extra money goes to
    the Visa first;
  - when it's paid off, its payment rolls to the line of credit;
  - payoff months and interest match hand-checked figures (table test).
- [ ] **Snowball:** the order is smallest balance first, and the comparison shows the extra
      interest against avalanche.
- [ ] **Rate line:** a debt below the person's line (a 4.5% mortgage, a 0% promotion) gets only
      its minimum, and the money goes to goals or "Save or invest".
- [ ] **Promotions:**
  - a balance transfer at 0% until a date is charged 0% until then, and its normal rate after;
  - its order position changes when the promotion ends.
- [ ] **Edge cases:**
  - a payment that never covers the interest says so and doesn't loop;
  - a debt with no rate goes last with "Add the interest rate";
  - nothing is guessed.
- [ ] **The split:**
  1. the buffer fills first, up to its goal's target;
  2. then the goal share goes to goals, up to each one's `required_monthly`, by priority;
  3. then extra goes to debt;
  4. then "Save or invest".

  The amounts add up exactly to the money left over. With a shortfall, the plan says there's
  nothing to split.
- [ ] **Choices:**
  - suggestions come from the household's own figures (buffer = one month of average spending);
  - every choice can be changed and is kept;
  - "no buffer" and a 0% or 100% goal share work.
- [ ] **Debts:**
  - type, rate, promotion, balance and account link are editable;
  - a balance older than 45 days and not from a statement shows a reminder;
  - an edit survives a later setup-wizard save;
  - another household's debt or account is 404.
- [ ] **Balances from statements:**
  - importing a card statement for a linked debt reads the closing balance and date, and
    updates the debt's balance (source: statement);
  - an older statement never overwrites a newer balance;
  - a missing or unreadable balance changes nothing and never fails the import.
- [ ] **Counting with links:**
  - debt paid counts only payments to the person's listed debts (a card paid in full can't hide
    a missed loan payment);
  - the Debt line leaves out a linked card's minimum when that card's statement is imported;
  - unlinked debts behave as today.
- [ ] **One source of truth:** the money left over equals the budget's Savings line for the same
      month.
- [ ] **Guidance only:** the disclaimer is linked from the plan; no product or security is ever
      named.
- [ ] **Backend checks:**
  - the migration applies, reverses and re-applies in CI;
  - `ruff` and `pytest` pass, and the `database` count grew.
- [ ] **App checks:**
  - Android and iOS match;
  - every string is in `i18n`;
  - `./gradlew ktlintCheck`, the Android build and tests, and the iOS `xcodebuild` pass, and the
    PR says so.
